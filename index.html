    Budgeting basics

    A budget is a plan for your money: what comes in, what goes out, and what you keep.

    Five words you will use every day
    TermMeaningStudent example
    IncomeMoney coming inAllowance, part-time pay, a scholarship
    ExpensesMoney going outBus pass, lunch, phone plan
    NeedsCosts you cannot skipRent, groceries, tuition, transport to class
    WantsNice to have, can waitConcert tickets, a third streaming app
    SavingsMoney set aside for laterEmergency fund, laptop fund

    Fixed and variable costs

    Fixed

    Same amount each month, so you can plan for it.

    Rent, phone plan, subscriptions.

    Variable

    Changes month to month, so this is where you have control.

    Food delivery, fuel, clothes, outings.

    Income sources

    Count only money you can rely on. Budget one-off gifts separately.

    Allowance, wages, freelance, gifts.

    Knowledge check

    Needs vs wants

    Sort each item. The answer often depends on the version: groceries are a need, a daily restaurant lunch is a want.

    Decision guide

    1. What happens if I skip it? If you lose housing, food, health, or school access, it is a need.

    2. Is there a cheaper version? Keep the need, downgrade the extras.

    3. Can it wait 30 days? If yes, wait. Many wants fade.

    Tip: write wants on a list and review it monthly.

    The 50-30-20 rule

    A starting guideline: half of income for needs, 30% for wants, 20% for savings. Adjust it to your life.
